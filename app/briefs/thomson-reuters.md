## 2. Incident Narrative & Timeline

### 2.1 Narrative arc

C-Track is a court case-management / e-filing oriented platform used by appellate and other courts to store and manage case documents and related records. Public notices attribute operation to **West Publishing Corporation** (US) and **Thomson Reuters Canada Limited** (Ontario). The platform is repeatedly described as **cloud-hosted** in Oregon and Ontario materials; Ohio’s Supreme Court notice adds an important local nuance that its appellate C-Track instance is **hosted by the <span class="redacted">Supreme Court of Ohio</span> and managed by TRCMS**, with TRCMS later telling the Court that unauthorized access occurred on the Court’s **<span class="redacted">production platform</span>** serving <span class="redacted">ten of twelve Ohio district courts of appeals</span>. Buyers should treat hosting topology as **jurisdiction-specific** and verify before asserting a single “TR cloud only” fact pattern for every named court.

According to the West/C-Track public notice reproduced by North Dakota courts and mirrored on **<span class="redacted">ctracknotification.com</span>**, C-Track discovered unauthorized third-party activity on **<span class="redacted">June 30, 2026</span>**, launched an investigation with third-party experts and law enforcement, and contained/secured the environment. The investigation found that in **<span class="redacted">March 2026</span>** an unauthorized party obtained certain C-Track files associated with the court systems listed in §3. Montana Chief Justice materials (via KTVH) state TR confirmed an unauthorized-access window of **<span class="redacted">2026-03-01 through 2026-06-29</span>** for Montana C-Track / e-filing **backup** data, implying dwell until discovery — TR’s broader public language still says files were obtained “in <span class="redacted">March 2026</span>,” and TR has not published a granular dwell forensic narrative for the public.

- <span class="redacted">Date of birth</span>  
- <span class="redacted">Health insurance information</span>  

Separately, notices state that **certain confidential, redacted, or sealed information may have been impacted for certain affected courts**.

### 4.2 Variability and sealed/redacted language

Exposure is **not** described as uniform. Nevada’s caution (via The Record) that data types vary by jurisdiction is consistent with how CMS platforms store mixed public docket text, party identifiers, and restricted filings. Montana commentary suggesting much affected information was already public, with some DL/DOB also involved, illustrates the same point: plaintiffs will plead the **worst available category in any jurisdiction**, while defense will insist on **court-specific inventories**.

Sealed/redacted/confidential language is the differentiator versus a routine SaaS HR or e-commerce breach. Even if the majority of bytes are public docket content, a non-zero sealed set can dominate reputational harm, judicial anger, and settlement leverage — especially where protective orders were meant to keep medical, juvenile, or safety-sensitive material from public view.

### 4.3 Downstream victims with no commercial relationship to TRI

Insurance Business and parallel analyses correctly stress that many potentially affected persons — litigants, witnesses, victims, persons merely **mentioned** in filings — never contracted with Thomson Reuters and never chose C-Track. Ontario’s chief justices explicitly include persons “mentioned in court documents.” That third-party-victim structure complicates standing, damages, and insurance response, and it expands the universe of sympathetic plaintiffs beyond TR enterprise customers.

### 4.4 What TR says was *not* hit

- **No evidence to date** of fraud or misuse of exposed information (company notices; Ontario statement on identity theft).  
- **No evidence** that systems used to process **financial transactions** were impacted.  
- **No operational disruption** to C-Track; platform stated safe to continue to use after added security measures reviewed/approved by outside experts (experts not publicly named — **UNKNOWN identity**).

**UNKNOWN.** Neither West/C-Track nor Ontario courts have published a verified unique-person count or record-volume figure as of this brief date. ## 6. Regulatory & Enforcement Matrix

### 6.1 United States — multi-state breach notification

Named US states and the USVI trigger a patchwork of breach-notification statutes with differing definitions of personal information, risk-of-harm thresholds, timing clocks, AG notice triggers, and substitute-notice rules. Ohio’s Supreme Court expressly framed its web posting as **substitute notice under <span class="redacted">Ohio Revised Code §1347.12</span>** while investigation continues — a signal that courts themselves are navigating controller/co-controller notice duties even when the technical incident is attributed to the vendor. Defense counsel for TR and for courts should build a **jurisdiction-by-jurisdiction notice matrix** (AG letters, consumer notice, media notice, tolling facts) rather than relying on the single <span class="redacted">ctracknotification.com</span> page.

### 6.2 Sealed court data issues

Sealed and confidential court records sit at the intersection of privacy statutes, judicial administrative rules, protective orders, and professional ethics. A vendor breach that implicates sealed material can provoke:

- Judicial administrative investigations or contract remedies beyond ordinary consumer PII claims;  
- Heightened AG and legislative interest;  
- Reputational theories that ordinary credit-monitoring offers do not cure (safety risks, doxxing of protected parties, compromise of sealed medical or abuse-related filings).

Oregon’s Flynn quote is the political and media embodiment of this track.

### 6.3 Oregon / OJD posture

OJD’s <span class="redacted">Sept 2</span> release is unusually direct for a judicial branch vendor incident: public transparency “out of an abundance of caution,” demand for vendor accountability, appellate-only scope clarification, and redirection of individual questions to the TR hotline and engagement number. Expect Oregon to remain a **message-discipline stress test** for any TR or court crisis script that sounds dismissive.

### 6.4 Ontario MAG + Canadian privacy (PIPEDA and provincial)

Ontario’s joint statement places MAG in the operational loop from <span class="redacted">July 23</span> onward and references consultation with Ontario government cybersecurity experts and the Cyber Security Division. Canadian response will be assessed under federal **<span class="redacted">PIPEDA</span>** principles (for appropriate private-sector custodians) and provincial public-sector / personal-health information regimes as applicable to court and ministry holdings. Exact statutory characterization of each data element and each custodian role is **fact-dependent** and should be mapped by Canadian privacy counsel; this brief does not invent that map. The public commitment to a **Canada-staffed** toll-free centre (active <span class="redacted">Sept 4</span> per the statement) is itself a regulatory-optics move.

### 6.5 Securities disclosure — SEC / SEDAR considerations (flag carefully)

TRI is a widely held dual-listed issuer.
